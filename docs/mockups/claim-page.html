“Crime is down 12 percent since this government came to office.”
Hear it — 1News At Six, 8 Sep 2026
48-second clip starting 14:22 · captions are YouTube auto-generated (Tier 2)
The number is right; the comparison chosen does most of the work. Recorded offence counts are 12% below the 2023 level. But 2023 was a pandemic-distorted peak: measured against 2019, the count is roughly flat, and the NZ Crime & Victimisation Survey — which captures offences whether or not they were reported — shows household victimisation essentially unchanged. A claim that is true as stated, but as deployed here the framing omits the baseline distortion and the reporting-rate effect, which are material to the conclusion a listener would draw.

Why this is "incomplete" rather than false

Every number in the claim is real and correctly sourced. What makes the verdict incomplete is selective framing: a listener would reasonably conclude crime has fallen materially since the government took office, when the defensible pre-pandemic comparison shows little change. Under this project's methodology (aligned to AVeriTeC's "conflicting evidence / cherry-picking" class) a claim is rated incomplete when the omission — not the assertion — is what misleads. Confidence reflects agreement across the two official series and the audit pass.
