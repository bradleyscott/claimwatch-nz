The mode section — five ways, plus two absences

Section 4 of the mode-aware trail is the only part that changes with the check. Everything else on the page — the sentences read, the check chosen, the sources, the second pass, where the check stops — is identical for every claim, because those are the same decisions whatever the evidence turns out to be.

A section renders when its mode's material exists, not merely when its mode was chosen. Two cases below show what the page says when a check ran but produced nothing to report: it names the absence. It never substitutes a generic paragraph for a mode's own material, and it never omits the fact that the check did not run.

How a figures check works — and what it did here

9 Sept · 9:20–9:35 am

The claim names a number and a period, so it is checked against the agency that keeps that record. One number is rarely one number: the same series is cut several ways, and every framing is computed and kept — including the ones that contradict the claim.

How the claim was read
A net migration figure, as an annual level, over Nov 2023 → Jun 2026, leaning on the Stats NZ series. One part is not stated: that the fall is because of the changes — and that implied cause is what the rest of the check tests.
Series used
Stats NZ net migration, vintage 13 Feb 2026 — the official record for this number, and the same series the claim's own figure comes from.

What this check cannot tell you

Whether the relationship is causal. It shows the timing of a fall relative to a policy — which is the strongest thing a series can say, and still not proof of cause. The verdict copy must not upgrade timing into cause.

Why the framing table is here at all

A check that only tests the framing the claimant chose has not checked anything. Rows that support the claim are shown too — the table is the whole computation, not the case against it.

How a citation check works — and what it did here

9 Sept · 9:18–9:26 am

The claim cites something that exists, so the check reads that thing rather than searching around it. The question is not whether the document is real — it is whether the claim uses it the way the document uses it.

The cited document
The 2023 coalition agreement, clause 12(b) — read in full, not from the claim's description of it.
What it says
“…to reduce net migration to sustainable levels” — with no figure and no date attached.
What the claim says it says
That the agreement “commits to cutting net migration by half”. That figure and that commitment appear nowhere in the document.
Binding or decorative
Not binding. The clause commits to a direction (“sustainable levels”), not to a number. A commitment to halve a figure would have been binding; this is not.
Decision
The claim misstates the document it relies on.
This is the strongest form of finding the pipeline can reach — the claim's own source contradicts it, so nothing outside the document is needed to decide.
binding no · mismatch recorded · paywalled no · instructions citation-compare@1
Where this check degrades — declared in use

If the cited document is paywalled or unreachable, the comparison cannot run. The page then says the claim was checked against the quotation alone, and the verdict is labelled as resting on that weaker basis. It does not silently fall back to a search.

Why the document is read in full

Claims about documents fail in the gap between a summary and its text. Reading the clause the claim leans on is the whole check — and the clause is quoted on the page so you can read it yourself.

How a quotation check works — and what it did here

9 Sept · 9:22–9:28 am

The claim reports what someone said, so it is checked against the recording itself. The first question is whether the words can be found at all: a quotation that cannot be located is not the same as one that was misreported, and the page distinguishes them.

Anchor
Found — 22 March 2026, 14:08, the Minister's reply in the House. The clip link is on this page.
What was said
“…we estimate the cost at two billion over four years” — the claim reports “two billion a year”.
How it was transcribed
Official transcript (Hansard), not an automatic caption — so the wording is checked against a reviewed record rather than a machine one.
Decision
The words are real; the figure's period is wrong.
A quote check can settle this exactly, which is why the verdict can be firm here and cannot be firm on a claim that needs a document nobody has published.
anchor found · tier publisher-reviewed · caption flag no · instructions quote-fidelity@1
Automatic captions are declared

Where the only record is an automatic caption, the page says the wording came from machine transcription and may contain errors, before it reports any finding. A quote check on a bad transcript is a quote check on the transcriber.

Why the clip is linked

The claim's own recording is the evidence. Linking to the moment lets you hear it rather than take our transcription on trust — the one check a reader can fully repeat without any of our machinery.

How a context check works — and what it did here

9 Sept · 9:24–9:31 am

Some claims are true of one event and attached to another. So the check asks what the claim is presented as, then tests whether the thing presented actually is that: the same date, place, document and subject. A claim can survive every word of this and still fail, because the attachment is the claim.

What it is presented as
A photograph of the protest outside Parliament, dated to the day of the vote.
What it is
The same photograph, published eleven days earlier at a different event. The image is genuine; the date attached to it is not.
How it was established
The image's earliest publication date, from the publisher's own page and an archived copy — the record of when it existed, not an estimate.
Decision
The content is real and the attachment is wrong.
Verdict copy here must describe the mismatch and the record it rests on — and never speculate about why the wrong date was attached. Reason is not evidence, and intent is not ours to grade.
claims as image, dated event · matched no · earliest publication 11 days earlier · instructions provenance-check@1
What this check must never say

That a mis-dated image was mis-dated deliberately. The record supports “this is not the event it is presented as”. It does not support a claim about why, and this is the mode where person-character language would be easiest to slip into.

Why the original is linked

The earliest publication is the evidence. Linking it lets you verify the date independently, which is the only part of this check a reader can confirm without our tooling.

Fires on: everything with no official series and no cited document

Why it needs the most scaffolding, not the least

Open-web is labelled on the page as our least reliable check — and it is the one most claims receive. The disclosure burden is therefore highest here: the questions asked, the searches run, whether the round cap bound, and every source the search found and we discarded.

The cap matters to the reader

“Cap reached: yes” means the search stopped because it was not allowed more rounds — a very different statement from “we looked and there is nothing published”. Conflating the two would let a budget limit read as a finding about the world.

The quotation could not be found

9 Sept · 9:22 am

The claim reports something said in an interview. No recording or transcript of that interview is published, so the words could not be located in any record — and a quotation check cannot run without the quotation's source.

This is not the same finding as “the quote was misreported”. It means the check did not happen, and the page says so instead of reporting a conclusion it cannot support.

Decision
No comparison ran. The verdict rests on the claim's own wording and nothing else.
Recorded as an absent check rather than a neutral one — a reader must be able to tell “we looked and it checked out” from “we could not look”.
anchor missing · comparison did not run · instructions quote-fidelity@1
The failure this rules out

A section that renders its scaffolding with empty values reads as a completed check. Where a check could not run, the page states the absence and what it means for the verdict's basis — the same rule the trail already applies to a step whose date the store does not hold.

Also an absence case

What this claim needs, and what we can check

9 Sept · 9:16 am

This claim is about cause: it says one thing made another happen. The checks we have can establish that two things happened in a certain order, or that a document says one thing and a claim says another — none of them can establish that one thing caused another.

So this page reports what is checkable in the claim and is explicit about the part that is not. The timing evidence is reported as timing, and the causal claim inside the sentence is left open rather than graded on a check that cannot reach it.

Decision
Report the checkable components. Do not grade the causal claim.
The verdict addresses the verifiable parts of the sentence and names the causal part as unassessed. It is not reported as “supported”, and not as “refuted” — either would claim a finding no check produced.
Checkable here
That the two events occurred in the stated order, and that a stated quantity changed between them — the timing rows a figures check would compute.
Not checkable with what we have
That the change was because of the first event. Testing that needs a comparison that was not affected by it, and no published evaluation provides one.
claim kind causal · mode none able to test the causal claim · components reported timing, quantity · causal claim unassessed
Why publish the gap rather than hide it

Roughly one claim in nine is causal, and the honest handling is to say which part of the sentence the check reaches. A page that grades the causal claim using timing evidence tells a reader something the record does not support — and does it silently.

What closing it would take

A sixth mode for causal claims — pre-trend tests, comparison groups, difference-in-differences against a series that was not affected — with its own section. Until then, the gap is stated on the verdict page and on /methodology, and it is a named gap in the spec rather than an untested assumption.
