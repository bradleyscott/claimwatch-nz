How this works, in this case

The same questions come up on every check. Here are the answers, using this claim as the example.

Why does each source have its own date?
Official figures are published in releases, and a release is only as new as its figures. The migration numbers we used were published on 13 Feb 2026 — about seven months before the claim. We say so because the same claim can be right against the figures available then and wrong against the figures available later.

We fetch the source again each time a claim is checked, and keep an archived copy of the version we used — so a verdict can always be traced to the exact figures behind it.
Who makes the call — and could a person change it?
The comparison against the official series is worked out by the automated system, then a second pass asks whether the evidence really supports the conclusion. Nobody writes the verdict by hand, and nobody can quietly overwrite one: a change to a verdict is a new version with a public diff and a logged reason. Before the 2026 election, verdicts are frozen from 5 Nov and reopen after the results are declared.
What would change this verdict?
Two things. A later release of the migration series — if revised figures moved the peak or the timing, the claim would be re-checked and the verdict could change. Or a contest pointing at a source we should have used. Both produce a new version of this page, keep the old one visible, and record why.
