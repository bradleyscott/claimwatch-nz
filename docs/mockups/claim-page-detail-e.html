1 · What the claim asserts

Six parts had to be pinned down before anything could be looked up. The parse decides which figures are the right ones — and one part of this claim is not stated at all.

“Since I got the job, net migration's dropped from 135,000 to around 24,000.”
PartHow we read it
1What it assertsNet migration fell from about 135,000 to about 24,000.
2Who is speakingErica Stanford, in her role as Immigration Minister — so the claim is also a claim about the government's own performance.
3The numbersAnnual net migration, in people, as a level. Not a rate, not a percentage, not per head.
4The period“Since I got the job” — no date in the sentence. Read as November 2023, the month she took office. This is a judgement call, and the framing table below shows what changes if you read it from the peak instead.
5The figuresStats NZ's published net migration estimates — the official series for exactly this number.
6Assumed, not saidThat the fall is because of the changes she made. The sentence is doing two jobs: reporting a number, and arguing for a policy. The number checks out; the argument is the part that had to be tested.

Why the sixth part matters most. A claim can be arithmetically true and still be misleading about cause. Splitting the sentence this way is what let us check the two halves separately — and it is why the verdict is neither a clean yes nor a no.

What we searched for — and what the search could not answer

The claim was broken into the questions evidence could answer, and searched for those rather than for the claim itself. Three questions, four searches.

The three questions
1. What was annual net migration in the year to November 2023? → answered by the official series.
2. When did the current downward trend begin? → answered, and it produced the row that decides this verdict.
3. Did the 2023 changes cause the fall? → not answered by anything published.

The third question is the one the claim's argument needs, and the one the record cannot settle. That is why the verdict stops at “incomplete” rather than continuing to “false”: the evidence shows timing, not cause.

3 · Where this check stops
What this check could not settle
  • Whether the fall continues. The series stops at June 2026; nothing after that is in the record yet.
  • What the policy itself did, separately from the trend already running. The figures show timing, not cause, and no published evaluation separates them.
  • The 2023 commitment. Tracked on its own page — the coalition agreement is evidence we cannot yet read in full.
What would change this verdict
  • A published evaluation attributing the fall to the 2023 changes — that would move the implied cause from unsupported to supported, or the reverse.
  • A revised vintage showing the trend began after November 2023. That would remove the one row this verdict turns on.
  • A recorded statement of the period intended by “since I got the job”. If a different start date was meant, the framing table is recomputed against it.
